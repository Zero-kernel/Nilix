## Executive Summary

This is the first full-tree audit since R187 (2026-08-05) and the KSA-2026-09-06 closeout. All five coverage partitions from `coverage-map.md` were audited line-by-line by independent reviewer agents against the current HEAD, with the driver performing cross-partition verification of every MEDIUM-or-above candidate and every cross-module dependency claim.

**Headline result: no new CRITICAL defects and no new memory-safety defects were found in any partition.** The codebase shows the accumulated effect of the R65–R189/RF178/RF180/KSA repair series: fallible-allocation discipline (`try_reserve`/`try_reserve_heap` everywhere user-controlled sizes reach allocators), PID-reuse generation tagging, lock-ordering documentation with enforced levels, exception-table usercopy with per-CPU SMAP guards, and fail-closed gates for FIPS/seccomp/audit/livepatch.

The single HIGH (R190-1) is a design-coverage gap, not an exploitable bug in current reachable code: 19 of 54 LSM hooks have zero call sites. The corresponding kernel features (ptrace, setuid, shm, chmod/chown) do not currently exist as syscalls, so there is no live bypass — but the SecureBaseline policy's promises for these areas are unenforceable dead code, and any future syscall added without hook wiring will silently bypass MAC. Two of the four MEDIUMs (R190-2 UDP socket byte accounting; R190-5 arch_prctl FS/GS high-half acceptance) are directly reachable today; the other two (R190-3 IRQ-context keyboard lock under SMP; R190-4 cpuset/delegate hook gaps) are conditional on SMP load or future API exposure.

- **Driver cross-checks (this round, independent of agents):**
  - IOMMU page-table frames vs 1 GiB direct map (agent lead I-2): **FALSIFIED as a bug** — `alloc_zeroed_page_table` enforces `phys_addr >= MAX_DIRECT_MAP_PHYS → free+fail` (kernel/iommu/domain.rs:573-581, R80-1) and `table_from_phys` panics fail-closed on any out-of-range/unaligned PTE (domain.rs:600-611, U29-4). Downgraded to verified-clean.

## Implementation Issues Found

### R190-1: 19 of 54 LSM hooks have zero call sites; SecureBaseline enforcement claims exceed the wired surface

**Severity:** HIGH
**Category:** Architecture / Security-Model Coherence
**Files:** `kernel/lsm/lib.rs` (definitions), `kernel/lsm/policy.rs:927-950` (dead SecureBaseline rules)
**Affects:** All configurations; material only to Secure profile claims
**Status:** FIXED, pending stage-3 review-fix (2026-10-18; systemic). Census correction: 20 zero-caller hooks (`hook_kpatch_load` only appears in comments). No syscall reaches IPC endpoint send/receive. Live surfaces are now wired: `hook_ipc_pipe`, `hook_ipc_futex`, `hook_net_control` (ARP learn/reply, ICMP echo/REJECT; RX is wired) and `hook_file_lookup`. Dormant families are listed in `lsm::FEATURE_ABSENT_HOOKS` with their syscall numbers. Hosted gates `lsm-hook-census` and the kernel-core dispatcher parity test enforce INV-SEC-02. SecureBaseline documentation is corrected. See the [fix summary](../fixes/R190-fix-summary.md) and [design](../design/d2-arc-1-security-surface-coverage-design.md).
**Related Design Finding:** D2-ARC-1

**Description:**

Full-tree census (driver-verified): 54 `pub fn hook_*` are defined in `kernel/lsm/lib.rs`; the following 19 have **zero** callers outside the LSM crate:

```
hook_file_chmod, hook_file_chown, hook_file_link, hook_file_lookup, hook_file_mmap,
hook_ipc_futex, hook_ipc_pipe, hook_ipc_recv, hook_ipc_send, hook_ipc_shm,
hook_kpatch_disable, hook_kpatch_enable, hook_kpatch_unload,
hook_net_control, hook_net_setsockopt,
hook_ptrace, hook_task_setgroups, hook_task_setresuid, hook_task_setuid
```

Consequences:
- IPC (send/recv/pipe/futex/shm) has **no MAC mediation at all** — unlike file/net/task paths, these hooks are absent from `kernel/ipc/*` even though the IPC syscalls exist and are reachable today. This is the live portion of the gap: `hook_ipc_*` protect *existing* attack surface, unlike the setuid/ptrace family.
- `SecureBaselinePolicy::ptrace` (policy.rs:943-950) and `task_setuid` (policy.rs:927-941) are dead code: the corresponding syscalls do not exist, so nothing is bypassed *now*, but the policy text promises enforcement the kernel cannot deliver.
- chmod/chown syscalls return ENOSYS/EOPNOTSUPP (`kernel/kernel_core/syscall.rs:18773, 23724`), masking the unwired `hook_file_chmod/chown`.
- kpatch enable/disable/unload hooks are unwired; only `kpatch_load` is delegated via `KernelOps::check_lsm_kpatch_load`. Currently backstopped by `SUPPORTED=false`.
- `cap_op::DELEGATE` (lsm/lib.rs:139) is defined but never used; `CapTable::delegate` (cap/lib.rs:1071-1109) enforces rights-monotonicity internally, but any future user-facing delegate syscall that forgets the hook will be invisible to policy.

**Security Impact:**
- Fail-silent expansion risk: any future syscall/subsystem added without hook wiring silently bypasses MAC with no compile-time or runtime alarm.
- IPC MAC claims in SecureBaseline are unenforceable today for live IPC syscalls.

**Suggested Fix:**
1. Wire `hook_ipc_*` into `kernel/ipc/*` send/recv/pipe/futex entry points (the only family protecting *existing* surface).
2. Add a mechanical coverage assertion: a boot-time or compile-time table mapping each syscall number to its required hook set, verified in `run_seccomp_cap_self_test`-style self-test; or a `#[cfg(test)]` census that fails when a `pub fn hook_*` has zero callers.
3. Document in `policy.rs` which SecureBaseline rules are currently aspirational (feature-gated) vs enforced.

### R190-2: UDP sockets have no per-socket byte cap; global cap (16 MiB) exceeds the 1 MiB heap budget

**Severity:** MEDIUM
**Category:** Resource Exhaustion
**Files:** `kernel/net/src/socket.rs:2106` (queue gate), `socket.rs:752` (`MAX_RX_QUEUE=64`), `socket.rs:759` (`MAX_GLOBAL_UDP_QUEUED_BYTES = 16 MiB`)
**Affects:** All configurations with UDP sockets bound
**Status:** FIXED, pending stage-3 review-fix (2026-10-18). Per-socket 128 KiB byte cap; global cap reduced to SocketPayload/2 (512 KiB) with const asserts (INV-RES-02). Correction: the heap is 2 MiB; 1 MiB is the SocketPayload class. Reassembled datagrams of up to 65,507 bytes let one socket exceed the whole class.
**Related Design Finding:** None

**Description:**

UDP RX queuing is metered only by packet *count* (64 per socket). Each packet may be up to the MTU (~1472 bytes payload), so one socket can pin ≈94 KiB — ~9% of the 1 MiB kernel heap. Roughly ten concurrently flooded sockets can exhaust the SocketPayload heap class and stall all TCP/UDP RX (fail-closed OOM, availability only). The nominal global byte cap (16 MiB) is 16× the physical heap and therefore never binding; real protection is incidental via the AdmittedVec heap-class OOM.

**Security Impact:**
- Remote UDP flood → heap-class exhaustion → network RX stalls (DoS, no memory-safety impact).

**Suggested Fix:**
Add a per-socket queued-bytes limit (e.g., 256 KiB, still ≥ heap budget only in aggregate with admission), or reduce `MAX_GLOBAL_UDP_QUEUED_BYTES` to a value consistent with the SocketPayload heap-class budget.

### R190-3: Keyboard/serial IRQ handlers take a blocking `spin::Mutex` (`KEYBOARD_BUFFER`) — SMP livelock window

**Severity:** MEDIUM (regraded from agent-proposed HIGH)
**Category:** Concurrency / IRQ safety
**Files:** `kernel/arch/interrupts.rs:2206, 2274` → `kernel/drivers/keyboard.rs:367-370, 393-394` (IRQ-side `KEYBOARD_BUFFER.lock()`), `keyboard.rs:420-438` (user-side `read_buf`/`read_buf_with_commit` holding the lock across a copy_to_user callback)
**Affects:** SMP only
**Status:** FIXED, pending stage-3 review-fix (2026-10-18; structural). All `KEYBOARD_BUFFER` access goes through `with_buffer` (IRQs masked, bounded). User copyout runs outside it under the consumer-only `KEYBOARD_CONSUMER`, so the VFS commit runs exactly once. The reviewer's duplicate-delivery finding against the first design is repaired and re-reviewed SAFE.
**Related Design Finding:** None

**Description:**

The documented safety premise (`keyboard.rs:354-357`) — "consumer uses `without_interrupts()` before locking" — closes only the *same-CPU* nesting window. Under SMP, CPU0's userspace reader can hold `KEYBOARD_BUFFER` across `read_buf_with_commit` (whose callback performs copy_to_user — non-trivial duration) while CPU1's keyboard/serial IRQ handler spins unboundedly in `lock()`. IRQ-context spinning on a lock held by a preemptible context is a classic livelock/latency amplifier.

**Security Impact:**
- SMP availability: IRQ backlog/livelock under coincident stdin reads + high-frequency keyboard/serial interrupts.

**Suggested Fix:**
IRQ side: `try_lock()`, on failure increment `DROPPED_BYTES` and set a deferred-drain flag (the `wake_stdin_waiters` R149-1 pattern already exists in-tree).

### R190-4: cpuset management API has no privilege layer and no LSM hook family

**Severity:** MEDIUM (pre-exposure requirement; no live syscall today)
**Category:** Architecture / Authorization
**Files:** `kernel/sched/cpuset.rs:235, 283, 320` (`cpuset_create/destroy/set_cpus` are `pub fn` with no permission checks)
**Affects:** All configurations once any syscall exposes cpuset management
**Status:** FIXED, pending stage-3 review-fix (2026-10-18; by construction). Management requires a `CpusetAdmin` token (host root or `CapRights::ADMIN`, or kernel context), and every operation is mediated by the new `hook_sched_cpuset`.
**Related Design Finding:** D2-ARC-1

**Description:**

Cpuset create/destroy/set_cpus validate only parent-mask subset and online bits. All tasks currently run in the root cpuset (`process.rs:3571`, id 0) and no syscall exposes these functions, so there is no live vulnerability — but the isolation primitive's entire authorization model is deferred to a future wrapper that does not exist, and no `hook_sched_cpuset_*` family exists in the LSM. Same root-cause class as R190-1.

**Security Impact:**
- Future exposure without a wrapper silently creates unprivileged CPU-partition management.

**Suggested Fix:**
Add privilege checks (or a `#[cfg]` gate) before any syscall wiring; extend the LSM hook census from R190-1 to cover sched.

### R190-5: `arch_prctl(ARCH_SET_FS/GS)` accepts canonical high-half (kernel) addresses

**Severity:** MEDIUM
**Category:** ABI / Hardening
**Files:** `kernel/kernel_core/syscall.rs:17086-17092` (`is_canonical` accepts `sign_extended == 0x1FFFF`), `17121-17143`, `17174-17191` (wrmsr to FS/GS base)
**Affects:** All configurations
**Status:** FIXED, pending stage-3 review-fix (2026-10-18). EPERM for any base ≥ `USER_SPACE_TOP`, as Linux does. CR4.FSGSBASE is cleared and asserted at boot.
**Related Design Finding:** None

**Description:**

The validation rejects only non-canonical addresses; any canonical high-half address (kernel half) is accepted into FS/GS base via wrmsr by an unprivileged caller. User-mode dereference still faults on U/S, so there is no direct read/write primitive, but: (a) it provides a fault-vs-no-fault probing oracle for kernel mappings (KASLR-weakening side channel); (b) if CR4.FSGSBASE is ever enabled, `wrfsbase` gives userland direct control; (c) downstream consumers of fs_base inherit an unconstrained value. Linux enforces `addr < TASK_SIZE_MAX` in `do_arch_prctl_64`; the equivalent low-half assertion is missing here.

**Security Impact:**
- Kernel mapping probe oracle; latent risk amplifier if FSGSBASE is enabled later.

**Suggested Fix:**
Reject `addr >= USER_SPACE_TOP as u64` in the `ARCH_SET_FS/GS` arms; document FSGSBASE-must-stay-disabled as a precondition in `cpu_protection.rs`.

### R190-6: NMI/panic UART output is not mutually excluded from IRQ-context UART writes

**Severity:** LOW
**Category:** Concurrency / Robustness
**Files:** `kernel/arch/interrupts.rs:801` (nmi_handler), `interrupts.rs:1892-1922` (first-tick `[T:X]` debug bytes from hard IRQ context)
**Affects:** All configurations (debug output path)
**Status:** FIXED, pending stage-3 review-fix (2026-10-18). Correction: the NMI handler emits nothing. The timer-IRQ `[T:X]` UART writer was removed.

### R190-7: VGA/framebuffer `_print` holds a plain `spin::Mutex` across `write_fmt`; panic/NMI print can self-deadlock

**Severity:** LOW
**Category:** Concurrency
**Files:** `kernel/drivers/vga_buffer.rs:164-188`, `kernel/drivers/framebuffer.rs:454-456`, `kernel/klog/lib.rs:154-156` (`klog_force!` chain reaches the locked `_print`)
**Affects:** All configurations
**Status:** FIXED, pending stage-3 review-fix (2026-10-18; structural). `with_display` uses the owner-CPU word as the lock (one compare-exchange). A same-CPU re-entry (IRQ, exception or NMI) skips the display; serial output is kept.

**Suggested Fix:** `try_lock` in `_print` with serial-only fallback, or a panic-context bypass flag.

### R190-16 (LOW): console-fd identity snapshot TOCTOU; `close(0/1/2)` semantics delegated entirely to callback

**Severity:** LOW
**Category:** ABI / fd lifecycle semantics
**Files:** `kernel/kernel_core/syscall.rs:11235-11244` (`lookup_console_file`), `12117-12137` (`sys_close`)
**Affects:** All configurations
**Status:** FIXED, documentation (2026-10-18). Correction: the close callback removes console fds like any other fd (EBADF when absent). Snapshot semantics are fdget-equivalent and documented in source.

**Description:** Console fast-path decisions use a one-shot downcast snapshot; a same-thread-group `dup2`/`close` can retarget the fd between snapshot and dispatch. Both paths ultimately re-resolve at the callback layer (with Z-4 clamp), so no memory-safety issue — only misdirected I/O. `sys_close` performs no fd-validity check of its own; `close(0)` may silently succeed if the callback does not cover console fds (table paths were covered by KSA-006).

**Suggested Fix:** Re-verify identity at the callback boundary for console fast-path, or drop the fast path; define close(0/1/2) semantics explicitly in the ABI doc.

### D2-ARC-1: Declared-vs-enforced security surface gap

**Design Severity:** D2 (High Risk)
**Pillar:** 2 — Architecture/Contracts
**Blast Radius:** cross-cutting
**Affected Subsystems/Entry Points:** LSM policy layer, IPC syscalls, cpuset (future), VFS mount/chroot privilege checks, cap delegate (future)
**Chainability:** 1-bug-away (any new syscall that forgets hook wiring)
**Irreversibility:** behavioral contract (SecureBaseline claims)
**Status:** IMPLEMENTED (verification pending, 2026-10-18). Census and wiring per R190-1/4/10; permissive-default threat model in `lsm/lib.rs`; the mount comment now matches the stricter uid-0 check. Residual leads: `sched_setaffinity`, cgroup 500-516, uid/gid_map writes, `audit_export`/`fips_enable`/KCOV have no hook family. [Design](../design/d2-arc-1-security-surface-coverage-design.md).

**Description:** The security model as documented (SecureBaseline rules, hook families, capability contracts) covers more surface than the implementation mediates. Individual instances are R190-1/R190-4 and the VFS root-only-vs-CAP_SYS_ADMIN contract mismatch (mount_namespace.rs:711 comment vs operations.rs:205/237/1032 root-only checks — the implementation is *stricter*, so the mismatch is documentation-level). The Balanced/Performance permissive default (lsm/lib.rs:483-484, 566-569) is an explicit zero-overhead design choice, not a defect, but is currently undocumented as a threat-model statement.

**Invariant(s) That Should Hold:**
- INV-SEC-02 (new): Every security-relevant syscall entry mediates through its declared LSM hook family, or the family is compile-time-annotated as feature-absent.
- INV-SEC-03 (new): The default policy object cannot claim enforcement for hook families with zero call sites.

**Proof Obligations (for code-level audit):**
- Mechanical census: for each `pub fn hook_*`, ≥1 external caller or an explicit `feature-absent` annotation.
- Boot self-test asserts the census.

**Evidence from This Round:** R190-1 census table; R190-4 cpuset; VFS contract mismatch (operations.rs:205 vs mount_namespace.rs:711).

**Related Implementation Issues:** R190-1, R190-4, R190-10

**Recommended Action:** Hook-coverage self-test + wiring `hook_ipc_*`; document the permissive-default threat model; align mount privilege comments with the stricter implementation (or implement CAP_SYS_ADMIN checks deliberately).

### D3-RES-1: Resource budgets denominated above physical capacity

**Design Severity:** D3 (Design Debt)
**Pillar:** 4 — Resource Model/Scalability
**Blast Radius:** subsystem (net)
**Affected Subsystems/Entry Points:** UDP RX accounting
**Chainability:** requires-many (flood + multiple sockets)
**Irreversibility:** internal-only
**Status:** IMPLEMENTED (verification pending, 2026-10-18). UDP caps reconciled with the SocketPayload class; const asserts enforce INV-RES-02.

**Description:** `MAX_GLOBAL_UDP_QUEUED_BYTES` (16 MiB) cannot ever be reached under a 1 MiB heap, so the *nominal* limit is decorative and the *actual* limit is the heap admission failure path. This inversion (real enforcement living below the declared limit) makes capacity reasoning misleading. R190-2 is the concrete instance.

**Invariant(s) That Should Hold:**
- INV-RES-02 (new): Every declared byte budget is ≤ the admitting heap-class budget, or is explicitly documented as a logical cap subordinate to admission.

**Related Implementation Issues:** R190-2

**Recommended Action:** Reconcile constants with heap-class budgets; add a unit test asserting cap ≤ budget.
